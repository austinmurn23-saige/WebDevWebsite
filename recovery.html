Recovery

Stretching
Stretching is foundational to the recovery process. After a lift, a swim, a run or just sitting at a desk for too long, it can take a large toll on your body. By stretching you are able to increase bloodflow to the areas in need of recovery, increase flexibility which decreases risk of injury and make sure that your joints are more comfortably set in their sockets. Before doing exercise, it is recommended to do dynamic stretches (Movements which mirror physical activity you are about to do with little to no weight). After excercise, however, it can be helpful to do static stretches (holds of the muscles you used).
Foam Rolling
In addition to stretching foam rolling can be super beneficial. It can help stretch out the fascia by compressing and decompressing target areas. It is especially helpful for the lower leg chain (Lower Back, quads, hamstrings and calves).
Hydration
Hydration is essential for not only recovery but preparing to do movement. In the same way that your car's engine needs oil, your body needs water. The role of water in the body is the transportation of nutrients to the muscles, your brain and tendons. Without water, you will feel sluggish, tired and your recovery will be stunted due to a lack of nutrients moving to the areas in need or recovery and preparation for exercise.
Nutrition
They say you are what you eat... it's super true! The foods which you eat contribute massivelt to the recovery process. Especially when it comes to the question of lifting, making sure you get enough protein for your muscles as well as carbs for you glycogen stores is super important to make sure your're not breaking down the muscle fibers for nothing. Fat is also super important for hormone production, nutrient absorbtion and rebuilding muscle tissue. It is best to make sure you don't overeat, because that can lead to cardiovascular issues as well as problems with mobility and insulin sensitivty. In the same vain, eating too little can cause issues with energy and strength.
Sleep
In the process of recovery, most of the heavy lifting is done when you hit the hay. When your body is in rest, that is when it produces most of its hormones and is able to most effectively repair the microtears invovled with working out. Different from eating, it's super hard to make up lost sleep. With food, you can just eat more calories that you missed, but your body needs to enter the sleep cycle, which has specific parameters and conditions to get "good sleep". The video below explains this really well but for a TLDR; 8 hours of sleep, cold room, minimal exposure to blue light, following circadian rhythm are the basics.
